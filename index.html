Sim vs live gaps

This paper book is deliberately conservative, but it still cannot capture everything a live wallet would face. If this were ever used with real money, treat the list below as hard limits of the simulation — not footnotes.

Latency competition

Paper entries wait at least one full poll after a token is first sighted, then fill at that later cycle's public quote. A live sniper would race at block zero / first-pool, and would also often lose that race to faster bots. This book never claims first-seen or zero-block fills.

MEV / sandwiching

Constant-product math uses the pool reserves shown on DexScreener at poll time. It does not simulate a sandwich, back-run, or any priority-fee auction around your tx. Live buys and sells can receive a worse price than this reserved-based fill.

Failed or dropped transactions

Every paper fill is assumed to land. Live Solana transactions expire, fail on slippage guards, or get dropped when the leader is busy. There is no retry-cost model here beyond a flat $0.03 base+priority fee on each simulated tx.

Honeypots and unsellable tokens

No public honeypot flag is available from the feeds this sim uses (pump.fun's API is Cloudflare-blocked; DexScreener quotes do not reliably mark mint/freeze/tax traps). If a later poll has no price or no reserves, the position is marked 0 after two missed cycles. A live buy might discover it cannot sell at all, including on the first attempt.

Quoted liquidity vs executable liquidity

Displayed base/quote reserves can be stale, include locked liquidity, or sit behind tax/transfer hooks. The sim treats those reserves as fully executable. Tokens that cannot be exited are supposed to go to 0, but a trap can still look liquid on a quote screen.

Prediction-market race and stale books

Beta will only take a fill if the CLOB book timestamp is fresh (≤45s) and only up to the displayed ask size, after taker fees. Paper still does not compete with other takers for that size, and a 15-minute cycle can miss an arb that existed for a few seconds. Directional 15m/hourly bets are placed only when this book itself observed a start-of-window spot; in-progress windows at init are skipped.

Fee and routing approximations

Solana tx fee is a conservative mid ($0.03 of the typical $0.01–$0.05 base+priority band), not a live priority-fee quote. Pump.fun / PumpSwap takes 1%; other AMMs use 0.25% (Orca 0.30%) plus a 0.10% USDC↔SOL routing haircut when the pool is not USD-quoted. Jupiter's exact route, tip, and impact can differ. Polymarket fees use each market's feeSchedule (crypto_fees_v2, rate × p × (1−p)); the CLOB /fees endpoint returned 404.

Gamma is a cash reserve, not a scavenger

There is no reliable public feed to simulate 'dead pool' scavenging honestly (recovering leftover liquidity, failed launches, or refund crumbs). Gamma's $200 stays in cash and only tops up Alpha or Beta when their cash runs low. That sleeve will not earn yield in this paper book.

Oracle and venue gaps

BTC/ETH 15-minute markets resolve on a Chainlink TWAP; the model uses Coinbase (Kraken/CoinGecko fallback) spot, not that TWAP. Binance was geo-blocked (HTTP 451) from this host and is not used. pump.fun coin lists were blocked (HTTP 403). Marks are always exit-after-slippage, never a mid you could not actually sell at.
